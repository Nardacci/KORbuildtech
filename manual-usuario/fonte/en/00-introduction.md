# Before you start

## What KORbuild is

KORbuild brings the work of estimating and following a job into one place: you measure the plans and build the proposal, release the project to the job, and receive the field's daily reports, all on the same data. This first version has three areas:

- **Takeoff:** measures the plans, calculates materials and labor, builds the schedule, issues the proposal and, in **Bids**, sends and receives bid invitations.
- **Daily:** the daily report (DDR). The foreman fills it in on the phone, the administrator approves it, and the progress goes into the schedule.
- **Settings:** the company, the people, what each person can do, the contacts and the roles with their hourly rates.

Other areas (time clock) are not released yet and do not appear for you.

## Who does what

Access is controlled by **roles**. Four come ready, and the company can create more:

| Role | For whom | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **Admin** | The owner or the person in charge of the account | Registers people, roles, contacts and job roles; sees and changes everything, including Settings (the field's daily report belongs to the foreman). This role is fixed: the company never ends up without someone who manages access. |
| **Project manager** | Whoever follows the jobs | Sees the modules and approves, without touching the settings: follows projects and daily reports, approves the field's report and measures in Takeoff. |
| **Foreman** | Whoever is on site | Fills in the day's report on the phone. (The crew's time clock comes with Crew, which is not in this version yet.) |
| **Worker** | Whoever does the work | Only clocks their own time, a Crew feature. In this version they have no tasks in the system yet. |

The administrator registers the people. Each person changes only their own personal data (name and phone) in **My profile**, never their own access role.

## How to use this manual

- Each chapter follows the path of a task, in the order it happens.
- Button and menu names appear in **bold**, as on screen.
- The images are real screenshots of the system, taken during the test run described in the last chapter. Names and values (company Northfield Builders, project Mitchell Garage Remodel) are examples.
- Daily was made for the phone. Its screens appear at iPhone size.
